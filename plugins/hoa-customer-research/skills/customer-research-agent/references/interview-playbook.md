# Customer Discovery Interview Playbook

The interview exists to learn how the customer behaves today, not to persuade them to like the product.

## Validate

- problem existence
- frequency
- operational impact
- current workflow
- current alternatives
- failure consequences
- user and buyer roles
- budget and purchasing process
- willingness to commit to a next step

## Recommended 20-minute structure

### Context — 2 minutes

Ask about their role, portfolio, and proximity to resident-support and maintenance workflows.

### Recent event — 5 minutes

Ask:

> Tell me about the most recent after-hours resident call or maintenance issue that required intervention.

Then ask what happened, who received it, what was collected, which systems were used, who was notified, how it was resolved, and what went wrong.

### Frequency and impact — 4 minutes

Ask how often this happens, which requests consume the most time, what happens when classification is wrong, what cost is created, and how residents or boards react.

### Current solution — 3 minutes

Ask how it is handled today, what tools or services are used, what works, what remains manual, and what they have tried.

### Buying and implementation — 3 minutes

Ask who owns the workflow, who approves a change, which budget pays, what integrations are required, and what legal or security concerns arise.

### Commitment — 2 minutes

Ask whether they would review a narrow prototype, share an anonymised workflow, or suggest another person.

## Avoid leading questions

Do not ask whether they would use an AI voice agent, whether the idea is good, whether automation would save time, or how much they would hypothetically pay.

Prefer questions about the last real event, current process, existing spending, approval history, and conditions for testing a different approach.

## Evidence classification

Separate observed fact, customer-reported fact, opinion, inference, hypothesis, contradictory evidence, and unknown.

## Commitment strength

From weakest to strongest: polite interest, detailed answers, follow-up questions, referral, workflow shared, data shared, prototype review, pilot agreement, paid pilot, contract or payment.
