# Personalisation Rules

Personalisation must explain why this particular person was selected. It must be truthful, relevant, public, and supported by a recorded source.

## Strong personalisation

Examples:

- the company explicitly offers 24/7 emergency support
- the person leads operations across multiple offices
- the company recently discussed resident communication or maintenance response
- the person spoke publicly about community-management staffing or service quality
- the company manages a clearly relevant portfolio

## Weak personalisation

Avoid generic praise, restating a title with no relevance, unrelated personal details, vague admiration, assumptions about internal problems, or intrusive information.

## Never fabricate

Do not invent referrals, prior conversations, mutual contacts, admiration, company size, customer counts, internal software, responsibilities, or email addresses.

## Source requirements

Every `personalisation_fact` must have a matching `personalisation_source`.

Preferred sources:

1. Official company page
2. Official leadership biography
3. Public LinkedIn profile
4. Official event or speaker page
5. Public company post
6. Reputable interview or article

## Writing rules

Use one concise sentence.

Good:

> I noticed that your team provides 24/7 emergency support across the communities it manages, which is closely related to the workflow I am researching.

Bad:

> Your impressive leadership and innovative company caught my attention.

## Fallback

When no meaningful person-specific fact exists, use verified company-level relevance. When neither exists, use the baseline template or reject the prospect.

## Privacy

Use only professional information needed for qualification. Do not use family details, personal contacts, political views, health information, sensitive demographics, or unrelated social activity.
