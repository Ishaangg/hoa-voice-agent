# Evidence Rubric

## Evidence strength

### Strong

- Current statute or regulation
- Government publication
- Official industry dataset with methodology
- Public company filing
- Direct product documentation
- Multiple independent customer interviews
- Direct observation of the workflow

### Moderate

- Reputable industry survey
- Established news publication
- Vendor documentation describing its own product
- Multiple consistent user reviews
- Detailed job descriptions
- Recorded product demonstration

### Weak

- Vendor marketing claim about the market
- Single customer quote
- Blog without methodology
- Unsourced market-size estimate
- Forum or Reddit post
- Search-result snippet
- Founder assumption

## Evaluation fields

For every important source record:

- publisher
- publication date
- date accessed
- geography
- segment
- sample size
- methodology
- commercial bias
- directness
- corroboration
- confidence

## Confidence levels

### High

Supported directly by multiple strong sources with no material contradiction.

### Medium

Supported by at least one strong source or several moderate sources, but with
limitations.

### Low

Based on indirect, anecdotal, old or conflicting evidence.

## Rules

- Vendor claims may establish what that vendor says or offers.
- Vendor claims alone cannot establish market prevalence.
- Reviews can identify possible pain points.
- Reviews alone cannot estimate pain-point frequency.
- Competitor existence does not prove customer demand.
- Market size does not prove obtainable revenue.
