# HOA Management-Company Responsibilities

## Service Stack

Authoritative descriptions from regulators and labour-market agencies show that a community association manager’s baseline remit includes **fund control, budget preparation, meeting administration, maintenance coordination, owner fee collection, contractor management, complaint resolution, record-keeping, and financial reporting**. Florida’s state licensing definition is especially clear: “community association management” includes controlling or disbursing funds, preparing budgets and other financial documents, assisting in noticing or conducting meetings, and coordinating maintenance and day-to-day services. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics adds inspections, bill payment, contracting with service providers, violation handling, budget preparation, and complaint resolution. citeturn22view0turn22view1turn30view0

In practice, large management firms publicly market a broader service stack. RowCal lists financial management, reserve studies, dues collection, reporting, accounts payable/receivable, bank reconciliation, tax filings, insurance coordination, bid management, governance support, bylaw enforcement, meeting coordination, compliance oversight, ARC/ACC coordination, election support, board education, portals, websites, resident support, vendor support, and a 24/7 emergency line. Associa publicly lists community management, maintenance, financial services, technology, developer services, real estate services, and lifestyle programmes. Sentry publicly lists assessments and enforcement, vendor relations, developer services, accounting and financials, onsite staff, homeowner communications, and board support. citeturn34view3turn43view1turn43view3

The strongest practical sales implication is that HOA management agencies are not merely “property managers”. They are **transaction routers, compliance stewards, and exception handlers**.
