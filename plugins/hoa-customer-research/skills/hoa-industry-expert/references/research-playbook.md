# HOA Research Playbook

## 1. Define the decision

Translate the founder's request into a decision.

Examples:

- Should we build this?
- Which customer segment should we target?
- Is this problem frequent enough?
- Who will pay?
- Which workflow should the product automate?
- Is a competitor direct or adjacent?

## 2. Define the unit of analysis

Specify:

- geography
- association type
- management model
- community size
- management-company size
- workflow
- stakeholder
- time period

Do not generalise findings from one state or company type to the entire US.

## 3. Inspect existing knowledge

Read relevant reference files.

Record:

- what is already known
- what is assumed
- what is stale
- what is missing

## 4. Gather evidence

Prefer:

1. Government and regulators
2. State statutes and regulatory agencies
3. Official community-association organisations
4. Public company filings
5. Official company documentation
6. Academic or institutional research
7. Reputable journalism
8. Vendor reports
9. User reviews, forums and community discussions

## 5. Triangulate

Do not rely on one source for commercially important conclusions.

Compare:

- official workflow descriptions
- management-company materials
- software documentation
- job descriptions
- customer reviews
- community discussions
- interviews
- product demonstrations

## 6. Separate evidence types

Classify each important item:

- Verified fact
- Reported claim
- Inference
- Hypothesis
- Anecdote
- Unknown

## 7. Look for disconfirming evidence

Ask:

- Is the problem actually frequent?
- Is it expensive enough?
- Is the buyer already satisfied?
- Does incumbent software solve it?
- Does the workflow require human judgement?
- Is adoption harder than expected?
- Is the addressable buyer segment too small?
- Are legal or integration risks prohibitive?

## 8. Form a conclusion

Include:

- direct answer
- confidence
- strongest supporting evidence
- strongest contrary evidence
- material unknowns
- next validation action

## 9. Preserve research history

Save substantial findings as dated reports.

Do not overwrite earlier reports merely because the conclusion changed.
